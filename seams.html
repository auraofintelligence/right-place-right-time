This scaffold was built in one pass while Luke was in the shower — deliberately, as an experiment. Everything that only he can decide is listed here instead of being guessed. The gaps are load-bearing: they mark exactly where his pen goes next.

Open questions

Only Luke can close these.

Purple means undecided — react, revise, refuse. Each one changes real copy on real pages when answered.

  • 1. The spine. What single sentence makes the whole body of work legible as one life's work? Four candidates from Luke's own repos wait in the open slot on the home page. Choose, blend or replace — the scaffold refuses to pick for him.
  • 2. The public/private boundary. A few public repos touching intimate territory are unlisted in this first pass. Whether they belong on a support-seeking front is entirely Luke's call — firewalling is an option to weigh, never a rule. The outside-reader risk read, plainly: patrons arriving cold form fast first impressions, and material that reads as private-life-adjacent can dominate those impressions before the work gets seen. Against that: unashamed is a stated value of this whole experiment, and curating it away has its own cost in honesty. His decision lands well either way; the site just needs to know which way.
  • 3. Audience per tier. Each tier page carries a provisional audience line (neighbours at the stall; Australians who want the work public; Pacific kin and hosts; patrons anywhere). Confirm or rewrite — the pitch and the proof shift with the answer.
  • 4. Amounts and rails. The support shapes are built; every number is deliberately absent. Patron tiers, sponsor-a-leg pricing, and which recurring rail to use (keeping commissions light, per the stall's pattern) all await Luke — with current Australian specifics verified live, never asserted from memory.
  • 5. The axes. Scale, horizon, portability, register — the four sorting axes on the register are a proposal. Confirm, rename or replace them before the remaining ninety-odd repos get shelved.
  • 6. The name. "Right Place, Right Time" is a working title lifted from Luke's own travel-strategy line. If it doesn't land, the repo forks cheaply — the core data file carries everything that matters.

Known seams

Facts already showing their threads.

Gold means checked against the repos, not guessed.

  • The music site is behind the music. The fourth album is complete at 24 songs, but the music universe site still shows it as upcoming with 13 songs mapped. Flagged for its own update pass — the "four albums deep" claim on this site follows the finished work, not the lagging page.
  • Odyssey numbers reconciled. An earlier brief said 250 territories over 10 years; the strategy page itself says roughly 256 over eleven years. This site follows the strategy page.
  • The repo registry exists. Scale-sorting 110 repos by hand was flagged as unreliable without a registry — good news: a human-curated audit and a machine-readable catalogue of 113 sibling repos already exist in the knowledge base and field library. Later sorting passes should consume those, not memory.
  • Payment links are placeholders. The only live checkout is the Strange but True catalogue, linked as-is. No payment link on this site pretends to work when it doesn't.
  • Existing fronts await reconciliation. iseeinfinity.com, lukecatalyst.com and the meet-luke page overlap with this site's person layer. Whether they fold in, link out or stay parallel is an open call.
  • The long life-log was quarried, not copied. The reflective log a layer down informed the person section but was treated as raw material only — Luke is an evolving being and this site refuses to freeze him to any snapshot.
