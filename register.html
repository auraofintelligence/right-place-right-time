The sorting logic

Four axes, confirm or replace.

Each project answers four questions. The axes themselves are up for revision on the seams page.

01

Scale

Local, Australia, Oceania or world — where the project inherently lives. One home shelf each, even when the pattern travels.

02

Horizon

Proven (live and evidential), active (live and growing) or horizon (described ahead of code, by design — unbuilt is not backlog, it is invitation).

03

Portability

In place (earns and functions only where it stands, like a market stall) or travels (music, writing, remote systems work). Portability matters more as scale widens.

04

Register

Record (gold, solid — established and citable) or proposal (purple, dashed — Luke's own provisional frame: react, revise, refuse).

What is not on this table, and why

Ninety-odd repos await sorting — and a boundary awaits a decision.

The full workshop holds around 110 public repos, already audited in the knowledge base (a human-curated shelf audit and a machine-readable catalogue both exist). This first pass shelved only the projects strong enough to carry a patronage pitch; the rest sort in later passes using those registries, not by hand from memory.

Separately: a small number of public repos touch intimate territory. They are deliberately unlisted in this first pass — not as a rule, but as a choice deferred to Luke. Whether they belong on a support-seeking front is his call alone, weighed with an outside-reader risk read on the seams page. Firewalling is an option, not a requirement.

Everything here is provisional and forkable — gold means proven record, purple means proposal: react, revise, refuse. Support returns something real, or it does not ship.
